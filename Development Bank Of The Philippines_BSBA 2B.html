Background & History

The Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP) was established in 1958 as a government-owned and controlled corporation (GOCC) to promote the economic development of the country. Initially created to provide financial assistance to industries and infrastructure projects, DBP has since expanded its services to support various sectors, including agriculture, housing, and social services.

DBP has played a critical role in the development of the Philippine economy by funding projects that contribute to long-term growth, improve infrastructure, and enhance the quality of life for Filipinos. As one of the leading development banks in the country, DBP focuses on sustainable growth, financial inclusion, and promoting social and economic resilience, especially in underserved and rural areas. The bank is committed to supporting government programs, including public-private partnerships (PPP) and green initiatives.

Over the years, DBP has continuously adapted to the changing needs of the economy, introducing innovative financial products, improving customer service, and ensuring that its operations are aligned with the country's development goals. Today, DBP is recognized as a major player in promoting national development and economic progress.

Corporate Profile

The Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP) is a government-owned financial institution dedicated to supporting the sustainable economic development of the Philippines. As one of the country's primary development banks, DBP's mission is to foster inclusive growth by providing financial support to key sectors such as infrastructure, agriculture, housing, and the environment.

Since its inception in 1958, DBP has been instrumental in financing projects that enhance national infrastructure, create jobs, reduce poverty, and improve the quality of life for Filipinos. With a focus on long-term development, DBP offers a range of financial products and services designed to help public and private entities, including loans, project financing, and investment solutions.

DBP operates under the oversight of the Department of Finance and works closely with government agencies, private sector partners, and international organizations to advance national economic objectives. With its strategic approach, DBP continues to contribute to the country's growth by supporting sustainable development, financial inclusion, and environmental initiatives.

Subsidiaries and Affiliates

DBP also has a number of subsidiaries and affiliates that extend its reach and impact in various sectors:

DBP Leasing Corporation (DBPLC)

A wholly owned subsidiary of DBP, DBPLC provides lease financing solutions to private and government entities, supporting the acquisition of equipment and other capital assets for business operations and development.

DBP Data Center, Inc. (DBPDCI)

This subsidiary is responsible for providing data management services, including data storage and disaster recovery solutions, to both public and private sectors.

DBP Insurance Brokerage, Inc. (DBP-IBI)

A subsidiary of DBP, DBP-IBI offers insurance services, acting as an intermediary for individuals and businesses seeking insurance products to safeguard against potential risks.

DBP Management Corporation (DBPMC)

DBPMC provides specialized asset management and investment services, helping manage and grow the bank's investment portfolio.

These subsidiaries and affiliates allow DBP to extend its financial and advisory services to a wider range of sectors, supporting not only infrastructure development but also a variety of business and government needs, reinforcing its position as a cornerstone of national development.

Capital Structure and Adequacy

The Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP), as a government-owned development financial institution, maintains a strong and stable capital structure that supports its role in funding critical infrastructure, agriculture, SMEs, education, and other priority sectors. Its capital structure typically consists of the following components:

Capital Structure

Authorized Capital Stock

₱35B

DBP's authorized capital stock is ₱35 billion, divided into common shares with a par value of ₱100 each.

A significant portion of this capital stock is subscribed and paid by the Republic of the Philippines, making it a fully government-owned institution.

Paid-up Capital

DBP's paid-up capital reflects the actual capital infused by the national government, and forms the core foundation of its lending and investment operations.

Equity Reserves and Surplus

Includes retained earnings, capital reserves, and revaluation surplus that have accumulated over the years from operations, investments, and asset appreciation.

Capital Adequacy

DBP complies with the Basel III framework as enforced by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), which sets standards for capital adequacy and financial resilience. Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) is a key measure of a bank's financial health.

Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR)

As of the latest reports, DBP maintains a CAR well above the BSP minimum requirement of 10% for government banks.

This ensures that the bank has sufficient capital buffer to absorb risks and continue lending even in adverse economic conditions.

Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA)

DBP regularly evaluates its risk exposure and adjusts its capital reserves accordingly to maintain a healthy balance between risk and capital.

Regulatory Compliance

DBP's capital structure and adequacy are regularly monitored by:

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP)

Commission on Audit (COA)

Department of Finance (DOF)

These agencies ensure that DBP remains solvent, stable, and capable of fulfilling its developmental mandate while safeguarding public funds.

DBP's sound capital base reflects its fiscal discipline, prudent risk management, and commitment to financial stability, all of which enable it to carry out large-scale lending programs that contribute to national development.

Corporate Social Responsibility

The Development Bank of the Philippines is dedicated to driving sustainable development and improving the quality of life for Filipinos through its various Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) initiatives. These programs reflect DBP's commitment to environmental stewardship, education, community health, and national progress.

DBP Forest Program

A comprehensive environmental initiative that has facilitated the reforestation of thousands of hectares across the Philippines. Through partnerships with local governments, indigenous communities, and environmental organizations, the program focuses on watershed rehabilitation, biodiversity conservation, and sustainable forest management practices.

DBP Endowment for Education Program

Provides scholarships, educational infrastructure support, and capacity-building initiatives for underprivileged students and educational institutions. The program aims to improve access to quality education and develop a skilled workforce that contributes to national development. It has supported thousands of scholars and numerous schools throughout the country.

DBP Lending Initiative for Sanitation (LINIS) Program

Addresses water access and sanitation challenges in underserved communities by providing financing solutions for water supply systems, wastewater treatment facilities, and solid waste management projects. The program promotes public health, environmental protection, and sustainable community development through improved water and sanitation infrastructure.

DBP Green Climate Fund

Mobilizes resources to support climate change mitigation and adaptation projects across the Philippines. The fund provides financing for renewable energy, energy efficiency, sustainable transportation, and climate-resilient agriculture initiatives. By channeling investments into climate-friendly projects, DBP contributes to the country's transition to a low-carbon, climate-resilient economy.

DBP Social Policy Statement

Guides the bank's operations and investments to ensure alignment with social and environmental sustainability principles. The policy promotes responsible banking practices, stakeholder engagement, and social impact assessment. Through this framework, DBP integrates social responsibility considerations into its core business activities and lending decisions.
